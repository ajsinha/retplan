One year of your plan

Every number has somewhere to come from.

Each year, income arrives and is taxed by the bands you typed; essential spending is paid before anything you would only like; the shortfall is drawn from the account that costs least in tax; what is left grows, pays its fees and is rebalanced. Then the books are checked: the closing balance must equal the opening one plus every flow. Every box in this figure is a row you can open in the cash-flow report - so the picture is not a metaphor for the model. It is the model.

How the model works

Sequence risk

Same returns. Different order. Different retirement.

Two retirees each start with a million and draw sixty thousand a year. They earn exactly the same thirty yearly returns - the same average - but one meets the bad years first. An average-return calculator shows them the same future. RetPlan shows you both, and how likely each is.

Capabilities

Not a calculator. A plan you can check.

A calculator gives you a number. A plan can say where every number came from, what it assumed, how likely it is - and what would change it.

The odds, with their error bar

A success probability from thousands of futures - with the plus-or-minus that comes from running a finite number of them - the bad-luck outcome, and the age the money runs out when it does.

What if, side by side

Copy the plan, retire two years later or spend a little less, and compare the versions on the same random seed - so the difference is the plan's, not the dice's.

Your real portfolio, in one upload

Drop in your broker's positions export. RetPlan finds the table, reads the columns, identifies every security by symbol, ISIN or name, and flags anything doubtful before it saves a thing.

Ten years out, or forty

Correlated Monte Carlo on your actual holdings - lognormal, fat-tailed or resampled from their own prices - with contributions, withdrawals and a goal, by year or by quarter.

History, replayed on your mix

2008, the dot-com bust, Covid, the 2022 rate shock and the 1970s: how deep, how long to recover - and what a crisis in your first year of retirement would do to the odds.

Any country's tax, exactly

Tax bands you type and account wrappers you define - a 401(k), an ISA, a TFSA, a pension - all one model, inverted exactly so "what must I withdraw to spend this" is never a guess.

The audit

Every year, the books balance.

A projection that loses a flow somewhere looks exactly like one that does not - until you check. RetPlan checks, every year of every simulated future: the closing balance must equal the opening one plus every contribution, withdrawal, fee and return. If it does not, the dashboard says so before it shows you a number.
