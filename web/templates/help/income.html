Common streams, and how to enter them

Salary

Category employment, real basis, growth 0.5–1.5% (real wage growth), from your current age to your retirement age, taxable 100%, survivor 0%.

State or social pension

Category state_pension, real basis, growth 0% if it is index-linked (the real basis already keeps it whole), from the claim age to 130. Set survivor to whatever your system continues — often 0% or 50%.

{% call note('tip', 'Testing a later claim') %}

Many systems pay more if you claim later. Model it by changing the start age and raising the amount by the actuarial uplift. Comparing the two runs is a genuinely useful exercise: deferring is usually the cheapest longevity insurance available.

{% endcall %}

Defined-benefit / final-salary pension

Category db_pension. If it has a capped escalation (say CPI capped at 3%), a real basis with 0% growth slightly overstates it in high-inflation paths; a small negative real growth is a reasonable approximation.

Rental income

Enter it net of voids, management and maintenance — this is the one place a net figure is right, because those costs are not modelled as spending rows. Keep the property itself on the accounts page, in an illiquid wrapper, so it is not sold to fund spending unless you intend it.

An inheritance or other windfall

Category one_off, from and to the same age, with a probability below 1 if it is uncertain. The sample plan carries one, switched off.

Annuity income

Enter the income it pays as a stream starting at the purchase age. The purchase itself — converting a balance into that income — is not modelled automatically; reduce the account balance by the premium to represent it.

{% call note('trap', 'The two ends of a salary') %}

If you change a retirement age on the household page and the numbers barely move, the salary row is still running. Its "To age" is independent, and the model will happily have you earning at 75.
