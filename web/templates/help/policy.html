The policy decides how much you take out each year and how that changes when markets misbehave. It is the second most consequential setting after spending itself.

The six rules

PolicyWhat it doesSuits
fixed_real Spends the same purchasing power every year, whatever markets do. The default and the baseline everything else is compared against. Maximum certainty of lifestyle, maximum risk of depletion.
fixed_nominal Spends the same number of currency units, so real spending falls with inflation. Rarely what anyone wants; useful to show what inflation does if you ignore it.
pct_portfolio Takes a fixed percentage of the current portfolio. Can never run out - but income swings with markets, which most people cannot live with.
vpw Amortises the remaining portfolio over the remaining years at an assumed rate. Spends more, later, than a percentage rule; deliberately runs the balance down to nothing by the horizon.
guardrails Fixed real spending, but cut after bad markets and raised after good ones. The practical middle. Usually supports a higher starting spend at the same risk.
table Divides the portfolio by a factor that falls with remaining years. Mimics a mandated minimum-distribution schedule.
{% call note('why', 'Why any flexible rule beats a fixed one') %}

A fixed real withdrawal has to survive the worst path you might land on, so it has to be set low enough for that path — which means spending far too little on the other 90% of them. A rule that cuts a little after bad years can start higher, because it no longer has to pre-fund the disaster. The cost is uncertainty in lifestyle; the benefit is usually thousands a year.

Choosing a confidence level honestly

A 90% plan and a 75% plan are not "safe" and "unsafe". They are different trade-offs between spending now and the risk of adjusting later. What should move the dial:

  • How much guaranteed income you have. If pensions cover your essential floor, a failure means cutting discretionary spending, not destitution — and a lower confidence level is rational.
  • How flexible you really are. See the split on the spending page, and be honest.
  • Whether you have a backstop — property to downsize, family, the ability to work again.

"Failure" in this model means the plan could not meet its need in some year, not that you end up with nothing. Look at the median total shortfall on the dashboard: a plan that fails at 94 by a small margin is a very different thing from one that fails at 72 by a large one.
