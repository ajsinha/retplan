    Adding and changing things

    Income, spending, debt, accounts, health and care, and conversions are shown as cards that read as sentences - "Salary: 95,000 a year, age 45 to 65". To add one, press Add and say what it is (a salary, a public pension, a mortgage…): a short dialog asks a few questions a step at a time, already answered with sensible values from your household, and keeps the rarely needed ones for a last, optional More options step. Click a card to change it; its menu can also duplicate it, pause it (leave it out of the plan without losing it - handy for "what if the inheritance never comes?") or remove it.

    If you would rather see everything at once, Table view shows every column of every row, for editing many rows together; press Save changes there before leaving.

    What to enter first if you only have ten minutes

    Four numbers carry most of the answer: your ages, your total annual spending, your total invested balance, and your guaranteed income (state and workplace pensions). Enter those four, leave everything else at its default, and the projection will already be roughly right. Refinement after that changes the answer at the margin, not in direction.

    {% call note('tip', 'Spending is where the accuracy is') %}

    People routinely know their portfolio to the nearest hundred and their spending to the nearest twenty thousand. A 10% error in spending moves the answer far more than a 10% error in expected return. If you only check one input twice, check this one.

    Two things the app does that will surprise you

    Everything is in today's money

    Unless a column says otherwise, every figure is in today's purchasing power. A balance of 2.6M in thirty years' time means "what 2.6M buys today", not the nominal number that will appear on a statement. This is deliberate: nominal figures flatter long-horizon plans and make them impossible to compare.

    Editing an input clears the simulation

    Change anything and the cached Monte Carlo results are dropped, because stale probabilities beside fresh inputs are worse than no probabilities. The deterministic projection — the verdict, the funded ratio, the charts on the left — recomputes on every page load and is always current.
