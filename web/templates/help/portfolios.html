A portfolio is a collection of accounts — everything you own and owe, in one place. Investment accounts hold real securities, priced every day from Yahoo Finance; cash accounts, property and debts are balances you keep up to date. Together they give your net worth, and the investable part of it is what the projection and stress tests work on.

You can keep as many portfolios as you like, and they are all equal. Most people keep one for the whole household; others are useful for what-ifs — a model portfolio, or a parent's money you look after. A retirement plan can be linked to a portfolio, so its accounts and debts always come from it.

Creating a portfolio

{% call steps() %}
  • Name it and choose its currency. The currency is the one you think in. Every value, gain and projection is shown in it; holdings quoted in other currencies are converted daily (see below), and cash, property and debts are entered in it. You can change it later from the portfolio's … menu.
  • Create and add accounts. The portfolio page opens, ready for its first account.
    • {% endcall %}

    Have a file from your broker listing several accounts? Start from it instead: {{ goto('builder', 'From a multi-account file') }} (see below). A portfolio can be duplicated (to try a different mix without touching the original) or deleted; deleting removes its accounts, holdings and saved projections, but the shared price history stays.

    Accounts

    Every account has a name, an owner (You, Partner or Joint), where it is held (an institution or lender, optional) and a type. The type says what kind of account it is and how it is taxed — taxable, tax-deferred, tax-free or not invested — which is what the tax-treatment view and a linked retirement plan need.

    KindTypesWhat it holds
    InvestmentsBrokerage, 401(k) / 403(b), Roth 401(k), Traditional IRA, Roth IRA, HSA, 529 plan, Pension pot (outside the US: SIPP, RRSP, super), Tax-free savings (ISA, TFSA)Positions, priced daily from Yahoo.
    CashChecking, Savings, CD / fixed term, Money market A balance you update by hand.
    PropertyHome, Other real estate, Vehicle, Other asset A value you update by hand. Counts in net worth, never in investable assets.
    DebtsMortgage, Home equity loan, Car loan, Student loan, Credit card, Other loanWhat is owed, the rate, the monthly payment and the years left.

    Adding an account

    {% call steps() %}
  • Add account on the portfolio page opens a dialog.
  • Pick the type from the tiles, grouped by kind.
  • Answer a few questions, a step at a time: its name, the owner and where it is held; for cash and property, its value; for a debt, what is owed, the rate, the years left and, optionally, the monthly payment.
  • A new investment account goes straight to its own page, where you add its holdings.
    • {% endcall %}

    To update a cash, property or debt account, open its card: the same dialog asks for the new value. Each card shows when it was last updated (updated 12 days ago), and a value more than 90 days old is flagged, as a nudge to look at it again.

    {% call note('why', 'Debts pay themselves down') %}

    A debt is paid down month by month from the date its balance was set, so it stays roughly right between updates. If you give no monthly payment, RetPlan uses the level payment worked out from the rate and the years left. Update the balance from a statement now and then and the clock starts again.

    Editing holdings

    The holdings table is one form. Change quantities, costs or classes in place, move a holding to another investment account of the same portfolio, mark rows for deletion with the bin, and press Save changes. Cost here is always the total. A symbol held in two accounts is two rows, one in each; the projection treats it as one asset.

    Building from a spreadsheet

    Upload the positions export your broker or bank gives you - an Excel workbook (.xlsx, .xlsm) or a CSV. Nothing is saved until you have reviewed it. The builder works in two ways:

    • Into one account. Upload positions on an investment account's page: every line goes into that account. You can choose to replace what it holds now; otherwise the lines are added to it.
    • A file listing several accounts - with an account column, or one sheet per account. Start it from the Portfolios page ({{ goto('builder', 'From a multi-account file') }}) or from a portfolio's … menu (Import a multi-account file).
    {% call steps() %}
  • RetPlan finds the table. The header row is found wherever it sits, below any title block, by matching cells against the words brokers use - Ticker Symbol, Description, Qty held, Market Value (USD), Avg Cost, Cost Basis, Account Name, ISIN and many more. Every sheet is read; with several sheets and no account column, each sheet becomes an account. Total and subtotal lines are skipped.
  • It works out the numbers. No quantity but a value and a price: quantity = value ÷ price. A cost column is read as a total when it is sized like the whole position and as per share when it is sized like one share. Cash, sweep and money-market lines become CASH for their value.
  • It identifies every security, most reliable first: the symbol as written (cleaned of NASDAQ:, US Equity or a trailing (AAPL) in the name), then an ISIN or other identifier looked up on Yahoo, then the name searched on Yahoo and matched by similarity.
  • It checks itself. Where the file states a price or value, it is compared with Yahoo's price times the quantity. A line out by more than a third is flagged; one out by about 100× is flagged as pence versus pounds (or cents versus dollars).
  • You review. Each line shows what the file said, the match and how it was made, and a confidence: certain, likely, uncertain or not found. Change a symbol (the alternatives it considered are offered), a quantity, cost or class - and, for a multi-account file, the account; untick anything you do not want. Lines that could not be found start unticked.
  • For a multi-account file, you confirm the accounts. Each account name found is listed with a type guessed from its words - Roth, 401k, Rollover, IRA, HSA, SIPP, Individual and so on. Check each type: it decides how the account is taxed in a retirement plan. Positions with no account go to one called Brokerage.
  • You choose where it goes: a new portfolio, or an existing one. Each account in the file becomes an account, or joins an existing account of the same name; the portfolio's other accounts are untouched. Prices arrive in the background a few seconds later.
    • {% endcall %} {% call note('tip', 'A security Yahoo does not know') %}

    A private fund or an unlisted share will come back not found. An administrator can add it as a manual security with typed-in prices (see {{ goto('help_prices', 'Daily prices') }}); then type its symbol into the line and tick it. A home or other real estate is better as a property account.

    {% endcall %}

    Old-style .xls files are not read - open them and save as .xlsx or CSV. The uploaded file itself is not kept; an unfinished review is kept for a week.

    Pasting from a spreadsheet

    Use Paste on an investment account's page. Paste rows copied from a spreadsheet or a broker's export, or choose a CSV file. Commas, semicolons and tabs are all recognised. Every row goes into that account.

    Without a header row, columns are taken in this order:

    symbol, quantity[, cost basis][, asset class]
VTI, 120, 21000
BND, 300, 22500, bond
CASH, 5000

    With a header row, columns are found by name, in any order, and extra columns (an account column among them) are ignored. The names recognised (case does not matter):

    ColumnHeader names accepted
    Symbolsymbol, ticker, code, security, instrument
    Quantityquantity, qty, shares, units, amount held, holding
    Cost basiscost basis, cost, book value, book cost, total cost, cost_basis, basis
    Asset classasset class, class, asset_class, type
    • Cost basis is always the total paid, not per share.
    • Numbers may contain thousands separators and $, £ or € signs; a figure in brackets such as (250) is negative.
    • An asset class must be one of the class names or keys in the table below (bond, Bonds, em_equity…); anything else is ignored and the class is detected automatically.
    • Rows without a symbol or quantity, or with an unreadable number, are skipped and listed by line number; the rest are imported.
    • Tick Replace this account's holdings to clear the account first; otherwise the rows are added to what is there.
    {% call note('tip', 'Broker headers usually just work') %}

    A header cell is matched against the names above exactly first, then by containing one of them, so "Ticker Symbol", "Qty held" or "Total cost" are recognised. Each column is used for one field only. If a column is still misread, rename the header or delete the header row and put the columns in the default order. For an Excel file, or an export that needs its securities identified, use Upload positions instead.

    Asset classes

    Every holding belongs to one class. The class supplies the long-run return and volatility the projection starts from, the default correlation with other classes, and the index the stress tests replay. The figures are round, deliberately conservative long-run nominal assumptions of the kind published in capital-market outlooks — a starting point, not a forecast. You can override them per holding on the projection page.

    A holding set to Auto follows the class RetPlan guessed for the security. To change it, pick a class in the holdings table, or use the form on the security's page, which sets it on all of your holdings of that symbol. Securities and their prices are shared by every workspace on the server, but a class you choose is stored on your holdings, so it never changes anyone else's.

    How the class is guessed

    When a symbol is first priced, RetPlan guesses its class from Yahoo's instrument type and the fund's name, checking in this order:

    1. Cash: the CASH symbol, a money-market type, or "money market" in the name.
    2. Crypto: a cryptocurrency type, or a symbol ending in -USD.
    3. Commodities: a futures contract, or gold, silver, commodity, oil fund or precious metal in the name.
    4. Bonds: bond, treasury, fixed income, aggregate, municipal, t-bill, tips, income fund, credit, govt, government, corporate, high yield or duration in the name — unless it also says equity.
    5. Property: reit, real estate, property or properties.
    6. Emerging equity: emerging or em markets.
    7. Intl equity: international, intl, ex-us, world ex, developed, europe, pacific, foreign, eafe, global ex or total intl.
    8. Equity: any other share, ETF, mutual fund or index.
    9. Other: anything left.

    Wrong guesses are cheap to fix. On a holding, the class menu shows Auto: … while it follows the guess; picking a class fixes it for that holding only. On the security's own page you can change the class for the security, which every holding set to Auto then follows. A global "World" fund that holds 40% outside the US is still classified as one class — pick the one that describes most of it.

    Currencies

    Yahoo quotes each symbol in its listing currency. When that differs from the portfolio's, RetPlan also collects the exchange rate (Yahoo's <FROM><TO>=X pair, such as GBPUSD=X) and converts every price with it:

    • The value uses the latest rate; the price history and the projection convert day by day, so currency swings become part of each holding's risk.
    • Some markets quote in minor units. London prices are usually in pence (GBp), so they are divided by 100 before conversion; the same is done for South African cents, Israeli agorot and Kuwaiti fils.
    • Until the rate has been fetched, the holding is shown unconverted and flagged no rate. Refresh prices fixes it.
    • Cost basis is not converted: enter it in the portfolio's currency.

    Target mix and rebalancing

    The target mix works on the investable assets, like the checks: cash accounts count as cash. Enter a target percentage for each asset class you want to hold and save. The targets need not add to exactly 100 — they are scaled to 100% — and a class left blank has a target of zero. The table then shows, per class:

    • Now, Target and Drift (now minus target). A class more than 5 percentage points from its target is marked off target.
    • Trade: what to buy or sell to be exactly on target, including any money you enter in Money to add (negative to withdraw).
    • New money only (when you add a positive amount): the new money split across the underweight classes in proportion to their shortfall, selling nothing. If nothing is underweight it follows the targets.
    {% call note('why', 'Why "new money only" matters') %}

    Selling in a taxable account can realise gains and a tax bill. Directing contributions to what is underweight rebalances more slowly, but for free — and for a portfolio still growing by contributions, it is often all the rebalancing you need.

    {% endcall %}

    Using a portfolio in your retirement plan

    A retirement plan can be linked to a portfolio. The link is live: every time the plan is read — the dashboard, the editor, a simulation — its accounts and loans are brought in line with the portfolio. A portfolio knows what you own and owe today; the plan adds what a portfolio cannot know: ages, income, spending, what you will save and in which order you will spend.

    Linking and unlinking

    • The portfolio page's Retirement plans using it card lists the plans linked to it, and offers Start a plan from it (the {{ goto('wizard', 'quick start') }}, with its accounts filled in) and Link the active plan to it.
    • In the plan, the Accounts and Debt pages show a banner: linked (with Unlink), or an offer to link to a portfolio. {{ goto('plan_section', 'Plan accounts', 'accounts') }}
    • Linking replaces the plan's own accounts and loans with the portfolio's.
    • Unlinking freezes today's figures as the plan's own; from then on they do not move with the portfolio.
    {% call note('tip', 'Linked accounts in the plan editor') %}

    A linked account's card carries a from portfolio chip. Its dialog asks only what the plan adds — what you save into it, the draw and pay-in order, rebalancing — and says that the balance, owner and investments come from the portfolio. A linked account cannot be removed from the plan: remove it in the portfolio, or pause it in the plan.

    The value chart

    A year of value prices today's investable assets - the holdings of every investment account, and the cash accounts - on each past day of the stored year. It answers "what would what I hold now have been worth", not "how did my portfolio perform" — RetPlan does not record trades, so it cannot know what you held last spring.

    Net worth over time is different: it is drawn from what was actually recorded each day (see {{ goto('networth', 'Net worth') }}), so it starts on the day the portfolio was first recorded.
