7% expected with 17% volatility does not compound at 7%. It compounds at roughly 7% − ½ × 17%² ≈ 5.6%. Check it: if the compound number is far below what you intended, your volatility is too high, not your return too low.

Correlations

A symmetric matrix with 1 on the diagonal. Only the upper triangle is editable; the lower half mirrors it. If the matrix you type is not mathematically valid (not positive semi-definite — easy to produce by hand), the engine shrinks it toward the identity by the smallest amount that makes it valid, and the dashboard reports how much it had to shrink. A large shrinkage means your matrix is telling an impossible story.

Regimes

A Markov chain over market states. Each year the market is in one regime, and the transition matrix decides where it goes next.

Crashes

A jump process layered on top, independent of the regime — so a crash can strike in a bull market, which is what actually happens.

Calibration — read this before changing regimes

Adding a bear regime and a crash process would, left alone, quietly lower every expected return you typed: the realised average would drift well below your input. With Calibrate to stated returns ticked (the default), the engine re-centres the regime drift and the crash drag so the long-run mean still equals what you asked for. Untick it only if you deliberately want the regimes to reduce your assumptions.

Distribution, inflation and fees

  • lognormal (default) cannot produce a return below −100%. normal can, and is floored. t adds fat tails with the same volatility — more extreme years, same average spread. Degrees of freedom 4–6 is a realistic range; below 2 the variance is undefined.
  • Inflation can be fixed, or stochastic with persistence and a correlation to equities (usually negative: inflation shocks hurt both stocks and bonds). Stochastic inflation matters most for plans with large nominal pensions.
  • Fees compound against you with total certainty. A 1% adviser fee over thirty years is roughly a quarter of the final portfolio. Enter them honestly: platform, adviser, and each fund's own cost.

What "mode" means

The projection you see on the dashboard always uses fixed returns — it is the deterministic backbone. The regime and crash machinery only runs when you press Run simulation. That is why editing a market assumption changes the charts immediately but the success probability only after a re-run.
