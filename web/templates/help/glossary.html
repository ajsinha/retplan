One line each. Where a term has its own help page, the definition links to it.

Money and time

Real / today’s money
An amount expressed in what it would buy today. The whole model works in real terms.
Nominal
A number of future currency units, which lose purchasing power as prices rise.
Basis
Whether an income or spending row is entered in real or nominal terms.
Inflation
The yearly rise in prices; simulated per trial, so each future has its own price path.
Horizon
How many years the projection runs, counted from this year.
Planning age
The age a person is planned to live to; after it, their income falls to its survivor share.
Spending smile
The tendency for real spending to fall through retirement and rise again late in life.
Essential floor
Spending you cannot cut; flexible policies never go below it.
Discretionary
Spending you could cut in a bad year.
Legacy target
Real wealth you want left at the end; success requires reaching it.

Tax and accounts

Wrapper
A tax treatment described as data: when money going in, growing and coming out is taxed. {{ goto('help_wrappers', 'Tax wrappers') }}
EET
Exempt in, exempt growth, taxed out — a typical pension or traditional 401(k).
TEE
Taxed in, exempt growth, exempt out — an ISA or Roth account.
TTE
Taxed in, taxed growth, exempt out — an ordinary taxable account (gains taxed on sale).
ETT
Exempt in, taxed growth, taxed out — rarer; some schemes and insurance bonds.
Account (ledger)
One pot of money in one wrapper, with its own balance, asset mix and contributions.
Draw order
The order accounts are used to fund spending.
Sweep account
The account any unspent income is paid into.
Forced draw (MRD)
A minimum distribution a wrapper requires from a given age, taxable and paid out whether needed or not.
Cost basis
What you paid for an investment; only the gain above it is taxable when sold.
Inclusion rate
The share of a capital gain that is added to taxable income.
Tax band
A slice of income taxed at one rate; an allowance is simply a 0% first band.
Gross-up
Working out how much to withdraw so that, after the tax it triggers, the need is met.
Effective tax rate
Total tax divided by income plus withdrawals.
Fiscal drag
Frozen tax bands that bite harder each year as incomes rise with inflation.

Markets and simulation

Asset class
A group of investments that behave alike (equity, government bonds, cash…), with one return, volatility and crash beta.
Expected return
The arithmetic average yearly return; compounding delivers less.
CAGR
Compound annual growth rate: the single steady rate that turns the start value into the end value.
Volatility
The standard deviation of yearly (or annualised) returns; the size of a typical surprise.
Correlation
How far two assets move together, from −1 (opposite) to +1 (in step).
Regime
A market state (bear, normal, bull) that shifts returns and volatility and persists from year to year.
Crash beta
How much of a market crash an asset suffers; negative means it gains.
Calibration
Re-centring returns so regimes and crashes do not quietly lower the averages you typed.
Monte Carlo
Running the plan against thousands of randomly generated futures and counting outcomes.
Trial
One simulated future.
Seed
The number that fixes the random draws; same seed and inputs, same answer.
Bootstrap
Building futures by resampling blocks of real past returns instead of drawing from a formula.
Standard error
How far an estimate from a finite sample may be from the truth; √(p(1−p)/n) for a success rate.
Success probability
The share of trials with no unmet need that also reach the legacy target.
Confidence level
The success probability you are aiming for; the solvers target it.
Percentile
The value below which a given share of outcomes falls.
P10 / P50 / P90
The 10th, 50th (median) and 90th percentiles: one in ten worse, the middle, one in ten better.
Median
The middle outcome; half are better, half worse.
Shortfall
Need in a year that the plan could not pay.
Depletion
The first year with a shortfall — “the money ran out”.
Drawdown
A fall from a previous peak, as a percentage; the maximum drawdown is the worst.
Sequence risk
The danger that bad returns arrive early in retirement, when withdrawals lock in the losses.
Funded ratio
Assets plus the present value of future income, divided by the present value of future spending.
Present value
A future amount discounted back to today at a chosen rate.
Deterministic
One projection with every return at its average — no randomness.

Withdrawal policy

Withdrawal policy
The rule that sets each year’s spending. {{ goto('help_policy', 'Withdrawal policy') }}
Guardrails
Cut spending when the withdrawal rate rises too far, raise it when it falls far enough.
VPW
Variable percentage withdrawal: spend a share of the portfolio that rises as the years left fall.
Withdrawal rate
A year’s spending as a share of the portfolio.
Glide path
An asset mix that changes with age, usually becoming more cautious.
Rebalancing
Selling what has grown and buying what has shrunk to restore the target mix.

The app and portfolios

Workspace
Everything that belongs to one browser: its plans and portfolios, found through an opaque id in the session cookie.
Scenario
One named version of your plan; a workspace has one active at a time.
Audit
The checks that prove the projection adds up. {{ goto('help_audit', 'The audit') }}
Holding
A quantity of one security in a portfolio.
Symbol
The ticker a price source knows a security by.
FX pair
An exchange rate between two currencies, priced like a symbol, used to value foreign holdings.
Time-weighted return
Growth that ignores when money was added or taken out — it measures the investments, not your timing.
Stress test
A historical crisis replayed on your current mix.
