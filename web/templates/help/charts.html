Every chart is drawn on the server as plain SVG, on a single y-axis, in today’s money unless it says otherwise. Most carry a Show the numbers link underneath that opens the same data as a table — use it whenever you want an exact figure rather than a reading off the axis.

Range of outcomes (the fan chart)

Net worth over time across every simulated future. Three nested bands in one colour, darkest in the middle:

  • the palest band spans the 5th to 95th percentile — nine futures in ten fall inside it;
  • the middle band spans the 10th to 90th percentile (the middle 80%);
  • the darkest band spans the 25th to 75th (the middle 50%);
  • the line is the median, labelled with its final value.

The table lists P5, P10, median, P90 and P95 every fifth year.

{% call note('trap', 'The median line is not a future anyone lives') %}

Each year’s band is a cross-section: the median in 2040 and the median in 2050 are usually different trials. Real paths wander from band to band — which is what the next chart is for. And the bottom edge at P5 is not the worst case; one future in twenty is below it.

{% endcall %}

Twenty individual futures (spaghetti)

The first twenty simulated trials drawn as faint lines, with the median on top. They are not chosen to be typical, just the first twenty. The point is the shape: two futures with the same average return can end far apart depending on when the bad years fell. The axis starts at zero, so a path that goes negative runs off the bottom. Its table lists the first five futures every five years, beside the median.

Where you end up (histogram)

Final net worth across all trials, in 28 equal-width bars from the lowest outcome to the 99th percentile; the top 1% are gathered into the last bar, and a note under the chart says how many, so one extreme future cannot flatten the rest. Hover a bar for its range and share. The distribution is nearly always skewed: a long tail of very rich futures to the right pulls the mean up, which is why the dashboard quotes the median. A tall bar at the far left is the pile of futures that ran out. Its table gives the percentiles P5 to P95.

Chance the money has run out

For each age, the share of trials that have already had a year with an unmet need. It only ever rises. Read it for timing: a curve that stays flat until 85 and then climbs is a longevity problem; one that rises in the first five years of retirement is a sequence problem. The table shows every fifth age.

Success against spending (the sweep)

Only after Full analysis. The plan is re-run at 17 spending levels from 50% to 150% of this year’s essential plus discretionary spending (debt payments are not scaled), and the success rate is plotted at each. The slope is the answer to “what does another thousand a year cost me in odds?” A steep section around your current spending means small cuts buy a lot of safety.

What moves the answer (the tornado)

Only after Full analysis. Each driver is moved down and then up on its own, everything else unchanged, and the plan re-run. The bar spans the two success rates, and its label shows the higher of the two. Drivers are sorted by the width of the bar, so the top one matters most.

{% call note('trap', 'Downside means moved down, not bad news') %}

In the table, Downside is the result with the driver lowered and Upside with it raised. For spending, fees or volatility, lowering the driver improves the odds, so the “downside” column holds the better number. Also note that the planning age does not change the length of the projection; it only moves the point at which survivor rules apply to income.

The deterministic charts

  • Net worth (before a run) — the single fixed-return path.
  • Where the money sits — a stacked area of the balance in each tax wrapper at the start of each year. Watch how the order you draw accounts down reshapes it.
  • Money in, money out — income, spending (including debt payments), tax, withdrawals and money paid in, one line each.

All three have tables, and the full year-by-year detail is in the reports.

Portfolio charts and hovering

The portfolio pages draw a projection fan, per-period return range bars, histograms of final value and worst fall, a year of value history and the crisis replays. Those charts carry a hover layer: move across them and a crosshair and tooltip give every value at that point. The dashboard charts do not hover — use their tables. How to read the portfolio fan and its yearly or quarterly table is in {{ goto('help_projection', 'Projecting a portfolio') }}, and the crisis charts in {{ goto('help_stress_tests', 'Stress tests') }}.

{% call note('tip', 'The table is the authoritative copy') %}

Charts round for legibility. When you want to quote a number, or compare two runs precisely, open Show the numbers. It is also the keyboard- and screen-reader-friendly way to read every chart that has one.
