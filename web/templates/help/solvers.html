The solvers turn the question round. Instead of “what are my odds?” they ask “what would I have to change to reach my confidence target?”. Each one re-runs the whole model — tax, forced draws, guardrails, draw order — at every step, so the answer respects everything the simulation does rather than a rule-of-thumb approximation of it.

How they search: bisection

Each solver has one knob and a range to search. It checks success at both ends of the range; if your target lies between them, it halves the range again and again, keeping the half where success crosses the target, until the range is narrower than a set tolerance. That is bisection: slow-ish, but it cannot be fooled by the noise in a Monte Carlo the way a gradient search can, and every step uses the same seed, so the success curve it walks along is smooth.

{% call note('warn', 'When the target is outside the range') %}

If success is above the target at both ends, or below it at both, there is nothing to bisect. The solver then returns whichever end is nearer the target and marks the answer as not bracketed. The dashboard does not show that mark, so treat an answer sitting exactly on a limit below (10% or 300% of spending, age 85, 200,000 a year) as “the target is out of reach within the range”, not as a precise figure.

The three solvers

Maximum sustainable spend

Scales every spending row — essential, discretionary and lumpy alike — by the same factor, searched between 0.1× and 3×. The answer is the first-year essential plus discretionary spending at the factor that just meets your confidence target. Debt payments are not scaled and not included in the figure, so it can be lower than the First-year spending tile, which does include them.

Earliest retirement age

Sets everyone’s retirement age to the same trial age, searched from the first person’s current age plus six months up to their planning age minus one (never past 85). Every employment and self-employment income row is cut off at that age if it would have run longer.

{% call note('trap', 'The solver can end a salary early, never extend one') %}

If your salary row stops at 60, trying a retirement age of 63 does not add three more years of pay — the row still ends at 60. If the earliest age looks stubborn, set salary rows to run to a late age and let the retirement age decide. Likewise, a couple with different retirement dates is solved as if both stopped together.

{% endcall %}

Extra saving needed

Adds a fixed real amount per year to the contribution of the account named in Surplus sweeps into account # on the accounts page, searched between 0 and 200,000. If the plan already meets the target the answer is zero. The money only goes in during working years with surplus income, and within that wrapper’s contribution cap — so a large answer can be one the engine cannot actually pay in. This figure is calculated by the full analysis but is not currently shown on the dashboard.

The sweep and the tornado

These are not solvers but ride on the same machinery. The sweep re-runs the plan at 17 spending levels from 50% to 150% of today’s spending. The tornado moves eight drivers down and up one at a time. What each perturbs is listed in {{ goto('help_charts', 'Reading the charts') }}.

Trial counts and accuracy

A full analysis runs the model dozens of times, so every solver, the sweep and the tornado use half your chosen trial count, capped at 1,500 and never below 200. At 2,000 trials that is 1,000 per evaluation, so each success figure inside a solver carries an error of about ±2 points. The headline odds use the full count. Choosing 10,000 trials for the full analysis buys a better headline, not better solver answers beyond 1,500.
