Every model simplifies. This page lists where RetPlan does, so you can judge whether the simplification matters for your plan. None of it is hidden elsewhere; it is collected here so you do not have to find it.

{% call note('warn', 'Not advice') %}

RetPlan projects the consequences of assumptions you supply. It does not predict markets, does not know your tax law beyond the bands you type, and its outputs are not financial, tax or investment advice.

{% endcall %}

Time and structure

  • Annual periods. The plan moves one year at a time. Timing within a year — a salary stopping in March, a monthly withdrawal — is not modelled. (Portfolio projections are the exception: they run quarterly.)
  • No random lifespan. The plan is funded for the full horizon you set; it does not draw an age of death. Planning age only decides when a person’s income falls to its survivor share.
  • Everyone retires together in the solver. The earliest-retirement solver gives every person the same age, and never extends a salary row beyond the age you entered.

Tax

  • Household-level tax. A couple’s income is taxed as one unit. Where individuals are taxed separately and incomes are uneven, the model overstates tax.
  • Allowances and tapers are bands. That keeps the schedule exactly invertible, but anything that is not a band — a credit, a means-tested benefit — must be approximated as one.
  • Capital gains are the gain share of a withdrawal times an inclusion rate, taxed under the ordinary bands. There is no separate gains schedule and no annual gains exemption.
  • The allowance is fixed by income before withdrawals each year; a withdrawal cannot taper its own allowance. The error is second order.

Markets

  • Your assumptions drive everything. Expected returns, volatility, correlations, regimes and crash parameters are yours. The model is as good as they are.
  • The deterministic view ignores diversification. By default the fixed-return projection grows each asset at its own typical (median) rate. A diversified portfolio compounds slightly faster than the average of its parts, so this errs a little on the cautious side. Choosing average on the Markets page instead errs on the optimistic side.
  • Income yield is informational. The taxable drag of dividends and interest inside a taxable account is not charged separately.

Features not built

  • Long-term care, annuity purchase, equity release, conversions between wrappers and bracket-filling optimisation. Care costs can be entered as a spending row with a probability; an annuity as an income row with the premium taken off a balance.
  • Monthly periods.
  • Stochastic mortality, as above.

Portfolios

  • One year of daily prices is kept. It estimates volatility and correlation well and the mean hardly at all, so expected returns lean on the long-run asset-class assumptions (blended with up to twenty years of monthly history where available).
  • Yahoo’s endpoints are unofficial. They can change, fail or throttle without notice. A symbol that cannot be priced counts as zero.
  • No transaction ledger. Holdings are a snapshot of what you own now. The “year of value” chart is a back-cast — today’s holdings valued on each past day — not a record of how your money actually performed.
  • Currency risk only as far as the stored year. Foreign holdings are converted day by day with the stored FX pair, so exchange-rate risk enters a projection only as it showed up in that history.
  • Crisis replays are approximate. They use rounded index paths, not a precise record of any fund.
  • No tax inside portfolio projections. Contributions, withdrawals and growth are projected pre-tax; for after-tax answers use the plan.

Where the answer will be wrong

Assume the figures are least reliable when:

  • a large share of wealth is in one illiquid asset, such as a business or a home you intend to sell;
  • tax depends heavily on rules that are not bands, or on two people’s separate assessments;
  • the plan relies on the exact month of an event;
  • the success rate sits within its error bar of your target — see {{ goto('help_simulation', 'Running a simulation') }}.
