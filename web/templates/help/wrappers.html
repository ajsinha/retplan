A wrapper is a description of when money inside it is taxed. Accounts hold balances; wrappers hold rules. That separation is what lets this model work in any country without a country setting.

The three decisions

Every tax-advantaged account in the world is some combination of three answers:

  1. Does putting money in reduce your taxable income?
  2. Is growth taxed as it arises?
  3. Is taking money out taxable?

Write E for exempt and T for taxed and you get the familiar shorthand:

EET100%100% UK workplace pension, US 401(k)/traditional IRA, Canadian RRSP, Australian concessional super
TEE0%0% UK ISA, US Roth, Canadian TFSA
TTE0%0% (but growth taxed yearly) Ordinary savings and deposit accounts

Worked examples

A UK-style pension

Deductible 100%, withdrawal taxable 75% (reflecting the 25% tax-free element taken across the drawdown), gains not realised, cap pct_income at your annual allowance, locked until 55–57, forced draws never.

A US-style traditional 401(k)

Deductible 100%, withdrawal taxable 100%, penalty 10% before 59.5, forced draws from 73–75, cap absolute.

A Roth or ISA

Deductible 0%, withdrawal taxable 0%, no penalty, no forced draws, cap absolute.

A taxable brokerage account

Deductible 0%, withdrawal taxable 0%, Realises gains ticked. The taxable fraction of each withdrawal is then the unrealised gain share of the account — 1 − basis/value — multiplied by the capital gains inclusion rate from the tax page. Set the cost basis on the accounts page; if you leave it equal to the balance, early withdrawals are treated as return of capital and taxed lightly, which is usually the right starting assumption. In a plan linked to a portfolio, the basis comes from the account's holdings instead: those with a known cost at cost, the rest at today's value.

{% call note('tip', 'Accounts from a portfolio') %}

When a plan is linked to a portfolio, each account's wrapper follows its type in the portfolio: a 401(k) or traditional IRA is taxed on the way out, with required withdrawals from 73 or 75; a Roth is tax-free; an HSA is tax-free, assuming it pays for health spending; a brokerage account taxes gains; cash accounts are cash; property is illiquid. A wrapper the plan lacks is added to it. Using a portfolio in your plan.

{% endcall %} {% call note('why', 'Why the draw order matters so much here') %}

Because wrappers tax withdrawals differently, the order you empty them changes the lifetime tax bill substantially. Drawing from a taxable account first while a pension compounds untouched is the common default, but it is not always optimal — filling the lower tax bands from the pension in the early retirement years, before forced draws and state pensions push you into higher bands, often beats it. The model will show you the difference: change the draw order on the accounts page and compare lifetime tax in the reports.

{% endcall %}

What is not modelled

  • Conversions between wrappers (Roth conversions and their equivalents) are not automated. You can approximate one with a withdrawal and a contribution in the same year.
  • Catch-up contributions exist as fields but are not editable in this web form; they are preserved from the plan JSON.
  • Growth taxed annually is carried on the wrapper but the web editor does not expose it; the sample data sets it correctly for cash and taxable accounts.
