The dashboard mixes two kinds of number, and the single most useful habit is to know which is which. Deterministic numbers come from one projection with every market return fixed at its average and inflation fixed at its mean. They are live the moment you save an edit. Simulated numbers come from the Monte Carlo and only appear after you press Run simulation or Full analysis. Everything is in today’s money.

The verdict

The large panel at the top answers one question. What it shows depends on whether you have run a simulation.

  • Before a simulation it reads Fully funded if the fixed-return projection meets every year’s need, or Funding gap if any year falls short, and names the age the money runs out.
  • After a simulation it shows the share of simulated futures that succeed, with its error bar (± 1.96 standard errors, a 95% interval) and the number of trials. A future succeeds when no year has an unmet need and the final net worth reaches your legacy target.

The sentence under the meter compares that probability with the confidence level you set on the policy page:

WordingRuleWhat to do
On trackSuccess is at or above your confidence target. Check the audit, then look at the bad-luck tile before relaxing.
CloseBelow the target by no more than 15 percentage points. Small changes — a year later, a little less discretionary spending — usually close the gap. Run the full analysis to see which.
Off trackMore than 15 points below the target. Something structural needs to change. The tornado chart shows which lever moves the answer most.

After a Full analysis the sentence also names the spending and the retirement age that would meet the target. See {{ goto('help_solvers', 'The solvers') }}.

{% call note('trap', 'Close is not a rounding error') %}

The band is fifteen points wide. A plan at 72% against an 85% target is “close”, but roughly one future in four still fails. Read the wording as “fixable with small changes”, not “fine”.

The stat tiles

TileSourceWhat it means
Money lasts to ageDeterministic The first person’s age in the first year the fixed-return projection cannot meet the need. If it never fails, the tile shows the age at the end of the horizon with a “+”.
Funded ratioDeterministic (Today’s portfolio + present value of future income from outside the portfolio) ÷ present value of future spending, discounted at the funded-ratio discount rate on the policy page. Spending here includes debt payments. 1.00 or more is shown in green.
Median final wealthSimulated if run, otherwise deterministic Net worth at the end of the horizon: the median across trials after a run, or the fixed-return figure before one.
Bad-luck final wealthSimulated only The 5th percentile of final net worth — one future in twenty ends worse. Turns red at zero or below.
First-year spendingDeterministic What the plan spends this year under your withdrawal policy, including debt payments.
Lifetime taxDeterministic Every year’s tax on the fixed-return path, added up in today’s money (not discounted).
Max sustainable spendFull analysis only The highest first-year spending that still meets your confidence target. Excludes debt payments.
Earliest retirementFull analysis only The earliest retirement age, applied to everyone in the household, that meets the same target.
{% call note('why', 'Why the funded ratio is not the success probability') %}

The funded ratio replaces the whole distribution of market returns with one discount rate. It is a quick, stable balance-sheet test: below 1.00 the plan needs returns above that rate just to break even. It says nothing about sequence risk, which is exactly what the simulation measures. Use the ratio to sanity-check, and the probability to decide.

{% endcall %} {% call note('warn', 'The fixed-return path is one future, not a forecast') %}

By default the fixed-return projection grows every asset at its typical (median) rate: the average return less the drag volatility causes when returns compound - 7% with 17% volatility compounds at about 5.6%. You can switch it to the arithmetic average on the Markets page, which makes every deterministic tile more optimistic. Either way it is a single smooth path; the simulation shows the spread.

{% endcall %}

Running from the dashboard

{% call steps() %}
  • Pick a trial count: 500, 2,000 (the default), 5,000, 10,000 or 25,000.
  • Press Run simulation for the odds and the outcome charts, or Full analysis to add the solvers, the spending sweep and the tornado. Full analysis takes several times longer.
  • Read the verdict and its ± figure. If the error bar straddles your target, run again with more trials before drawing a conclusion.

    The panels below the charts

    What you are actually assuming sets the returns you typed beside the ones the simulation produced after regimes and crashes, plus the compound (geometric) figure — the effective mean less half the variance. It is computed on a separate sample of between 500 and 4,000 futures. Market regimes shows each regime’s long-run share, the share simulated and its expected length, with the worst drawdown across all trials and the median total shortfall. The charts themselves are covered in {{ goto('help_charts', 'Reading the charts') }}.
