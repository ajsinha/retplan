How the schedule works

The level payment is computed once from the opening balance, rate and term, the standard annuity formula. Each year the model charges interest on the outstanding balance, applies the payment plus any overpayment as principal, and carries the remainder forward. The final year clears whatever is left, so the balance lands exactly on zero rather than drifting.

Debt service appears on the dashboard inside first-year spending and in the cash flow report as its own column. The outstanding balance is subtracted from net worth.

Pay it off, or invest?

Paying down debt earns a guaranteed return equal to the loan rate, after tax and with zero volatility. Investing earns an uncertain return. The comparison that matters is the loan rate against the portfolio's expected return after fees and the tax on the account the money would go into — not against the headline return.

To test it properly, see the recipe: the money must come from the same place in both runs, or you are comparing two different plans.

{% call note('why', 'Why clearing debt helps the bad paths most') %}

A mortgage is a fixed obligation. In the futures where markets disappoint, that fixed payment is a larger share of a smaller portfolio, and it forces selling at the worst time. Clearing it does not just change the average outcome; it removes a liability specifically from the paths where you can least afford it. That is why the success probability often improves more than the median terminal wealth suggests it should.

What is not modelled

  • Variable rates. The rate you enter is fixed for the term. For a tracker, either enter a rate you consider a fair long-run average, or run the plan twice at the ends of a plausible range.
  • Refinancing. Model it as the loan ending and a new row starting in the relevant year.
  • Interest deductibility. Interest does not reduce taxable income. Where that matters (some rental structures), reduce the net rental income instead.
