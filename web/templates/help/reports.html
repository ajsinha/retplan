Open the reports

Three year-by-year tables — cash flow, balance sheet and tax — plus the audit on the last tab. They are where you check the model’s arithmetic against your own understanding of your finances, one year at a time.

What the reports show, and what they do not

  • Deterministic. Every report is the fixed-return projection: each asset earns its typical (median) growth rate every year - or its average, if you chose that on the Markets page - and inflation sits at its mean. It is the same path behind the deterministic dashboard tiles, not a median of the simulation. Running a simulation does not change these tables.
  • Real terms. Every figure is in today’s money, so a flat line means “keeps its purchasing power”.
  • One row per year, starting with the current calendar year. The age column is the first person’s age.
{% call note('why', 'Why a deterministic table at all') %}

A median across thousands of futures is not a path: its income, tax and balances come from different trials and do not add up. The fixed-return projection does, so it is the only table you can reconcile line by line. Use it to check the mechanics, and the simulation to judge the odds.

Cash flow

ColumnWhat it holds
IncomeEvery income row for the year, plus any forced draws.
Forced drawsMinimum distributions a wrapper mandates from a certain age, shown separately so you can see them (they are already inside Income).
SpendingWhat the withdrawal policy set for the year, plus debt payments.
TaxTax on income plus the extra tax triggered by withdrawals.
WithdrawalsGross amounts taken from accounts to cover the gap, in draw order, before tax and any early-access penalty.
Paid inContributions and any surplus swept into an account.
FeesFund, platform and adviser charges taken from the balances.
ShortfallNeed the plan could not meet. Any row with a shortfall is shaded red.
PortfolioTotal account balances at the end of the year.
{% call note('tip', 'The line that should always balance') %}

For any year: Income + Withdrawals − Tax − Spending + Shortfall = Paid in, apart from any early-access penalties. If you have none and a row does not balance to the nearest unit, something is wrong — check the audit.

{% endcall %}

Balance sheet

One column per tax wrapper, then the portfolio total, the debt outstanding and net worth (portfolio less debt).

{% call note('trap', 'Two different moments in one row') %}

The wrapper columns are balances at the start of the year; Portfolio and Net worth are at the end of it, after that year’s flows and returns. So the wrapper columns of one row add up to the Portfolio of the row above, not the row they sit in.

{% endcall %}

What to check: that the draw order empties the wrappers you expect first; that a mortgage disappears in the year it should; that net worth does not fall off a cliff the year a pension or salary stops.

Tax

ColumnWhat it holds
Taxable incomeThe taxable base for the year: taxable income above the allowance, including the taxable share of withdrawals.
TaxTotal tax, as in the cash-flow report.
WithdrawalsGross withdrawals, as in the cash-flow report.
Effective rateTax ÷ (Income + Withdrawals). Blank in a year with essentially nothing coming in.

The effective rate is the number that describes what it costs to fund a year. Look for spikes: a year where a pension starts on top of a salary, or where forced draws push you into a higher band, is often cheaper to smooth by drawing earlier from the taxed wrapper. What to check in each

{% call steps() %}
  • First row. Does income and spending match what you actually live on this year? If not, fix the inputs before looking at anything else.
  • Retirement year. Does the salary stop and withdrawals start in the year you expect?
  • Pension start ages. Does income step up when each pension begins?
  • The first shortfall. If any row is red, that is the “money lasts to age” year on the dashboard.
  • Tax spikes. A jump in the effective rate is a planning opportunity.
