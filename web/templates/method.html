How it works

The parts worth understanding before you trust a number.

Everything is computed in today's money

Nominal figures are produced by multiplying by each trial's own realised inflation path, so a "real" result is correct path by path rather than deflated by an average. Tax bands are handled by homogeneity: a progressive schedule scaled by s satisfies Tax_s(x) = s · Tax_1(x/s), so indexed bands need no adjustment in real terms and frozen bands — the fiscal drag case — are exact with s = 1/CPI.

Tax inverts exactly

The naive way to answer "how much must I withdraw to spend X after tax" is a circular reference. Because a stacked progressive tax is piecewise linear and strictly increasing, it can be inverted in one pass instead: find the band the answer lands in by comparing the net achievable at each band bound, then solve within that band. Penalties fold into the same solve by substitution, and a partially taxable withdrawal is just a different slope.

Markets come in three layers
  • Regime. A Markov chain over bear / normal / bull, each with its own drift, volatility multiplier and correlation tightening — because diversification fails exactly when you need it.
  • Diffusion. Correlated shocks via a Cholesky factor, drawn lognormal, normal, or variance-matched Student-t for fat tails.
  • Jumps. An independent crash process: a Bernoulli occurrence, a triangular depth, a multi-year drawdown shape, per-asset transmission betas (bonds can be negative and gain), and partial recovery.

Adding regimes and crashes would otherwise quietly lower every expected return you typed, so the engine re-centres the drift and the crash drag to hold the long-run mean at your input. The dashboard reports what the simulation actually produced next to what you asked for.

Random numbers are reproducible

L'Ecuyer's combined generator, seeded per trial and per dimension, so the same seed gives the same answer on any machine, changing the trial count does not reshuffle unrelated draws, and a single trial can be reproduced alone. RAND() appears nowhere.

What "success" means

Terminal real wealth at or above your legacy target, with no year in which an essential need went unmet. The probability is reported with its standard error, because "82%" from 200 trials is really 82% ± 5 and a false precision is worse than no number.
